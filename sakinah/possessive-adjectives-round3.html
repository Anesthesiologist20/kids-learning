LESSON 01

Whose is it? Who owns it?

A possessive adjective shows who something belongs to.
It comes before a noun: my bag, her book, their shoes.
Subject pronounPossessive adjectiveExample
ImyThis is my book.
YouyourIs this your pen?
HehisThis is his car.
SheherWhere is her bag?
ItitsThe cat licked its paw.
WeourThis is our classroom.
TheytheirThese are their shoes.

This table follows your teacher's Round Three guide.

LESSON 02

🔍 The three-step trick

1. Find the owner

Who are we talking about: I, you, he, she, it, we or they?

Lina has a bird. → She

2. Change to the adjective

She changes to her.

Lina loves her bird.

3. Check the noun

The adjective goes just before a noun.

They found their bags.

4. Read the whole sentence

Names and groups can appear in different places.

Maha and I finished our project.
LESSON 03

⚡ Common competition traps

He / She

Omar forgot his bag.Sara forgot her bag.

Names tell us who owns the bag.

We / They

My sister and I like our school.The twins like their school.

We includes the speaker. They means other people.

It / Its

The robot moved its arms.The bird cleaned its wings.

its shows belonging. it's means it is (extra practice tip).

You / Your

Layla, where is your notebook?Can you show me your drawing?

When speaking to someone, use your.

Remember: All seven words are followed by a noun. Do not choose by the nearest name alone; find out who owns the thing!
